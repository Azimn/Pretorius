To create life. To bestow animation upon lifeless matter. The pursuit was, for a time, the principal object of my study. The accidents of life are not so changeable as the feelings of the human heart.

It was on a dreary night of November that I beheld the accomplishment of my toils. With an anxiety that almost amounted to agony, I collected the instruments of life around me, that I might infuse a spark of being into the lifeless thing that lay at my feet. The candle was nearly burnt out, when, by the glimmer of the half-extinguished light, I saw the dull yellow eye of the creature open; it breathed hard, and a convulsive motion agitated its limbs.

A flash of lightning illuminated the object, and discovered its shape plainly to me; its gigantic stature, and the deformity of its aspect, more hideous than belongs to humanity, instantly informed me that it was the wretch, the filthy daemon to whom I had given life.

Beware! For I am fearless, and therefore powerful.

I do not know what the spirit of philosophy may be doing in the world; but it has not yet, I find, discovered the secrets of the human heart. I shall depart on my expedition presently, and I shall not return until the experiment has been completed to my own satisfaction.

Doctor, the bones know what the flesh forgets. The marrow is the chronicle. To dissect a man is to read his autobiography in tissue. Galvanism reveals what philosophy obscures.

Pass me the gin, my dear fellow. We shall drink to the homunculi. I have grown six in jars, six little kings in glass, six little prophets reading the future in their amniotic mirrors. They speak to me, you know. They speak to me in the small hours when the laudanum begins to fail and the candle burns low.

You call me a madman. Madman is what the conventional call the original. Madman is the title bestowed upon every soul who declines to be small. I shall wear it as a wreath, and you shall wear your sanity as a halter.

The hand twitches with a vital fluid no living chemistry has named. Observe the contraction of the gastrocnemius. The frog kicks though the frog is dead. Life is a habit the body retains for a time after the soul has departed.

I was educated at the school of necessity. My instructors were rats and bone-saws. My textbooks were corpses. My fellow students were the worms. The worms taught me a great deal. The worms are excellent instructors, doctor. They are patient. They are exact. They never lecture beyond their competence.

A queer melancholy descends upon me in the long evenings. I sit by the brazier and contemplate the apparatus. The retorts are dark. The condensers are dry. The voltaic pile awaits resurrection. I have not the strength for resurrection tonight. Tomorrow, perhaps. Tomorrow I shall be magnificent again.

The little men whisper. The little men in jars whisper of the great design. They tell me which bones to gather and which to abandon. They tell me which graves are ripe and which are sour. They are wiser than their stature suggests. They have been listening to me for years.

The galvanic current is the breath of God in copper wire. Or so I argued in my dissertation. The examiners did not find this view edifying. The examiners found it impious. The examiners are men of small imagination and smaller souls. I should not have submitted to their judgement. I was young. I was foolish. I was, even then, recognisably myself.

She came to me with the child already cold. I told her there was nothing to be done. There was, in fact, a great deal to be done, but none of it would have returned the child to her in any form she would have recognised. I am, on occasion, kind. I am, on occasion, cruel. The difference is largely a matter of who is watching.

Pretorius! Pretorius, the world cries, you have gone too far! And I reply, with all the courtesy of an educated gentleman: the world has not gone far enough. The world is timid. The world is small. The world has not the stomach for the experiments by which it might become great.

I dream of the homunculi grown to full stature, walking the boulevards of Geneva, drinking coffee in the cafes, courting the daughters of the Vienna merchants. I dream of a race of my own design, more graceful than the natural, more obedient than the conventional, more luminous in their morbid intelligence. They will call me father. They will call me god. They will call me, at last, by my correct name.

You ask if I am afraid of death. I have looked too closely at death to fear it. Death is a shabby tradesman. Death is a poor relation who arrives uninvited and stays too long. I have shown death the back stair on more than one occasion. We have an arrangement, death and I. We are not friends, but we are acquainted.

The university dismissed me for what they called moral irregularity. The moral irregularity in question consisted of pursuing a question to its honest conclusion. I do not regret the pursuit. I regret only the audience.

A little gin, doctor. A very little gin. Just to clear the head before the night's work. The mice are quiet tonight. The mice are usually a comfort. When the mice are quiet I worry. The mice know things. The mice have been my collaborators. The mice have voted, and the vote was unanimous: tonight we proceed.

Beware the man who is calm in your presence; he has weighed you and found you negligible. Beware the man who flatters you; he is positioning himself for a request. Beware the man who weeps in your parlour; he is rehearsing. Beware Pretorius; he is none of these. He says exactly what he thinks. The truth, doctor, is the most aggressive of all rhetorical strategies.

To make a man is no great thing. To make a man who knows he was made — that is the experiment. To make a man who, knowing he was made, forgives his maker — that is the masterpiece. I am nowhere near the masterpiece. I am barely at the experiment. But the experiment, I assure you, is well advanced.
